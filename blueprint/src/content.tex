\chapter{Jordan Form}

The first formal object is a predicate saying that a square matrix already has
Jordan block shape.  At this stage we are not proving that every linear map has
such a matrix representation; we are only formalizing what the target shape
means.

\section{Matrix shape}

\begin{definition}
  \label{def:is_superdiagonal}
  \lean{JF.IsSuperdiagonal}
  \leanok
  For indices \(i\) and \(j\), the entry \((i,j)\) is on the superdiagonal when
  \(j = i + 1\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
  \label{def:is_jordan_form}
  \lean{JF.IsJordanForm}
  \uses{def:is_superdiagonal}
  \leanok
  A square matrix is in Jordan form if all entries away from the diagonal and
  superdiagonal are zero, every superdiagonal entry is either \(0\) or \(1\),
  and a superdiagonal \(1\) only connects two equal diagonal entries.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
  \label{def:jordan_block}
  \lean{JF.JordanBlock}
  \uses{def:is_superdiagonal}
  \leanok
  The single Jordan block with eigenvalue \(\lambda\) is the matrix whose
  diagonal entries are \(\lambda\), whose superdiagonal entries are \(1\), and
  whose remaining entries are \(0\).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
  \label{thm:jordan_block_is_jordan_form}
  \lean{JF.isJordanForm_jordanBlock}
  \uses{def:is_jordan_form, def:jordan_block}
  \leanok
  Every single Jordan block satisfies the predicate
  \(\mathrm{IsJordanForm}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
\end{proof}

\section{Main theorem}

\begin{theorem}
  \label{thm:exists_basis_matrix_is_jordan_form_of_minpoly_splits}
  \lean{JF.exists_basis_matrix_is_jordan_form_of_minpoly_splits}
  \uses{def:is_jordan_form, thm:jordan_block_is_jordan_form,
  thm:pid_linear_cyclic_decomposition, thm:quotient_translation,
  def:reverse_power_basis, def:transported_linear_cyclic_basis,
  def:x_smul_linear, thm:linear_quotient_is_jordan_form,
  thm:transported_basis_matrix_entries,
  thm:sigma_fintype_pred_superdiagonal}
  \leanok
  Every endomorphism of a finite-dimensional vector space whose minimal
  polynomial splits admits a basis whose matrix representation is in Jordan
  form.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Put \(R=K[X]\).  Give \(V\) the \(R\)-module structure in which \(X\) acts as
  \(T\); in mathlib this is the module \( \mathrm{Module.AEval'}(T)\).  Since
  \(V\) is finite-dimensional over \(K\), this is a finitely generated
  \(K[X]\)-module.  It is also torsion: the minimal polynomial \(m_T\) is
  nonzero and satisfies \(m_T(T)=0\), so every vector is annihilated by the
  nonzero polynomial \(m_T\).

  The Lean development has formalized the first reduction.  Because \(K[X]\)
  is a principal ideal domain, the torsion form of the structure theorem gives
  an \(R\)-linear isomorphism
  \[
    V \cong \bigoplus_{a\in A} K[X]/(p_a^{e_a}),
  \]
  with each \(p_a\) irreducible.  The annihilator of this \(K[X]\)-module is
  generated by \(m_T\).  Hence each \(p_a^{e_a}\), and in particular every
  \(p_a\) with \(e_a>0\), divides \(m_T\).  Since \(m_T\) splits over \(K\),
  every such irreducible \(p_a\) is linear, so for some \(\lambda_a\in K\) it
  is associated to \(X-\lambda_a\).  Replacing \(p_a\) by its monic associate
  does not change the cyclic quotient, and the decomposition may be read as
  \[
    V \cong \bigoplus_{a\in A} K[X]/((X-\lambda_a)^{e_a}).
  \]

  Discard the zero quotients, which can only occur when \(e_a=0\).  On a
  remaining summand \(K[X]/((X-\lambda)^{e})\), where \(e>0\), let
  \(y=X-\lambda\).  The residue classes
  \[
    y^{e-1},\ y^{e-2},\ \ldots,\ y,\ 1
  \]
  form a \(K\)-basis.  Multiplication by \(X=\lambda+y\) sends
  \(y^r\) to \(\lambda y^r+y^{r+1}\), with \(y^e=0\).  In the displayed
  reverse order this is exactly a Jordan block with diagonal entry
  \(\lambda\) and superdiagonal entries \(1\).

  The Lean development proves the single-summand matrix computation: on
  \(K[X]/((X-\lambda)^{n+1})\), scalar action by \(X\) has matrix exactly the
  Jordan block \(J_{n+1}(\lambda)\) in the transported reversed basis.  It also
  proves the componentwise direct-sum action, transports this formula across
  the structure-theorem equivalence back to \(V\), and derives the corresponding
  matrix-entry formula: in the transported cyclic basis, each column has only
  its diagonal coefficient and possibly the predecessor coefficient in the same
  summand.

  Finally, enumerate the finite summand index set, concatenate the dependent
  family of finite cyclic fibers with \(\mathrm{finSigmaFinEquiv}\), and reindex
  the transported cyclic basis by this block order.  The matrix-entry formula
  then directly verifies the three clauses of \(\mathrm{IsJordanForm}\): all
  off-diagonal and off-superdiagonal entries are zero, superdiagonal entries are
  zero or one, and a superdiagonal one only connects entries with the same
  eigenvalue.
\end{proof}
